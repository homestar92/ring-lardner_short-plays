A Friend of Frank's

Characters.

  • Frank M. Schulte, an Outfielder.

  • Heine Zimmerman, a Bear Wherever You Put Him

  • Si Simpson, a Recruit

  • George Pest, a Cub Fan.

  • Joe Bug, another Cub Fan

Scene—Old Chi and Tampa, Fla. Time—March

Scene I

A ginmill in old Chi. Pest and Bug stand with their feet on the rail.

A ginmill in old Chi. Pest and Bug stand with their feet on the rail.
Bug Some cold tonight.
Pest Some cold is right. I'll be glad to get away from here.
Bug Goin' away?
Pest Yes, I'm beatin' it to Florida tonight. Goin' to drop in on the Cubs at Tampa.
Bug Gosh, I wisht I could go along. Know any o' the Cubs personally?
Pest Sure! I got a lot o' pals on the team. Johnny Evvers and Frank Chance was two o' my best friends. Course they're both of 'em gone now, but I know Tom Needham and Larry Cheney and Roger Bresnahan and Heine Zimmerman and Frank Schulte—
Bug Do you know Schulte?
Pest Do I know Schulte? Say, him and I is just like brothers. I call him Wildfire.
Bug Does he know you're comin'?
Pest No, I'm fixin' to surprise him.
Bug I'll bet he'll be glad to see you.
Pest Will he be glad to see me? Say, he'll pretty near kiss me. I can have anything he's got.
Bug Well, I hope you have a good time. Good luck and please to of met you.
Pest Same to you. So long, and maybe I'll run acrost you when I get back to dear old Chi.

Scene II

The lobby of the hotel at Tampa. The athletes are lolling around. Enter Pest. He approaches Schulte.

Pest Are you one o' the Cubs?
Schulte Me? No. I work for one o' the Chicago papers.
Pest O, is that so? Tribune?
Schulte No, I'm Mr. Fish of the Journal.
Pest Pesase to meet you Mr. Fish. Is Schulte and Zimmerman anywheres around?
Schulte I think they have gone to church. No. Here comes Schulte now.
Enter Zimmerman
Pest He looks bigger in his citizen's clo'es.
Schulte I didn't get your name. Pest? Mr. Pest, shake hands with Mr. Schulte.
Pest and Zimmerman shake hands.
Pest Please to meet you Mr. Schulte. I've often watched you play. I've often wanted to meet you. I wanted to ask you how you hit them home runs.
Zim O, I'm some hitter all right. But I wisht I could hit 'em like Zimmerman.
Pest I think you're a better hitter than Zimmerman. He strikes out a good deal.
Zim Yes, but he does it on purpose If he didn't strike out oncet in awhile no pitchers wouldn't pitch agin him.
Pest What was the matter with him last year?
Zim O, him and Evers didn't get along. He knowed so much more about managin' a ball club that Evers got jealous.
Pest Any truth in the story about him and Evvers havin' a fight?
Zim Sure they was. Zimmerman give him an awful beatin' Then Cheney and Bresnahan and Needham and Archer cut in, and Zimmerman licked all of 'em. Then they called in four or five policemen and Zimmerman kicked 'em all out the door.
Pest I'd like to meet this guy Zimmerman.
Zim You better keep away from him when he gets mad.
Schulte There he comes now. I'll interduce you.
Si Simpson enters and is hailed by Schulte.
Simpson Whaddaya want?
Schulte Mr. Zimmerman, shake hands with Mr. ⸻. I didn't get your name? Pest? Shake hands with Mr. Pest.
Simpson and Pest shake hands.
Pest Please to meet you, Mr. Zimmerman. I've often saw you play.
Simpson What's the gag?
Zimmerman Gag? Where do you get that stuff. Heine?
Simpson Tryin' to kid somebody?
He passes on.
Pest What's the matter with him?
Schulte He's always like that He's got the swell head.
Pest Is he sore at me?
Schulte O, Heine's sore at everybody.
Pest I've always heard he was a peculiar guy, but I never knowed he was so bad as that. You could tell by the way he acts on the field that he's swell headed.
Zim He ain't no more swell headed than some o' them bugs up in the stand nor bone headed, neither.
Pest What's the matter, Mr. Schulte? You talk like you was sore at them bugs.
Zim You bet I'm sore at 'em, and if you're one of 'em you'd better beat it before you get hurt, you bum.
Exit Zimmerman
Pest Gee! He got pretty sore, didn't he? I always heart that Schulte was a good natured, quiet cuss.
Schulte Schulte? You never can tell nothin' about him. You know he has epileptic fits, and he's just as li'ble to shoot you as look at you. If I was you I'd beat it, or he might jump you when he comes back.
Pest Well, let's you and I go somewheres and get a drink.
Schulte I'd like to go with you but I've got to write some stuff for my paper. I have to wire in ten thousand words yet tonight.
Pest Well, I may see you later. When you see Schulte tell him I didn't mean nothin', and I'm sorry he got sore at me. I'm beatin' it back to old Chi tomorrow. I expected to be here a week but they wired me to hurry home. So long, Mr. Fish.
Schulte So long, Mr. ⸻. I didn't get your name. Anyway, so long.

Scene III

A cigar store in old Chi. Bug is leaning against the counter. Enter Pest.

Bug Well, are you back in old Chi?
Pest Yes, I'm here again. Had a great time, and seen all they was to see.
Bug Did you get to Tampa?
Pest Sure I was there a week. I had some time with the Cubs. Say, but that's a nice bunch o' fellahs.
Bug See your friend Schulte?
Pest Sure. Him and I was out together pretty near every night.
Bug Did you meet any o' the rest o' the boys?
Pest O, I knowed 'em all except the recruits before I went down there. We all went out together pretty near every night and lifted a few. They're good fellahs. They ain't a swell headed one in the gang. Zimmerman's peculiar, but he's good hearted. Schulte was tellin' me all about how Zimmerman licked Evvers last year.
