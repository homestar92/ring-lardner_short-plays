Casual Encounters

Being a faithful report, based on the recent statements of the principals, of the conversations that took place following the accidental meetings, in St. Louis and Chicago, of Ban Johnson, president of the American league, and Phil Ball, president of the St. Louis Feds.

Act I

Scene—Jim's Place, St. Louis

Phil Your face is familiar, but I can't recall the name.
Ban Johnson. Ban Johnson of Chicago.
Phil O, yes; Dan Johnson.
Ban No. Ban, not Dan.
Phil What business are you in, Mr. Johnson.
Ban I'm connected with the American baseball league; president of it.
Phil Well, that's a coincidence. I'm connected with baseball, too. I'm Phil Ball, part owner of the Federal league club here.
Ban O, is that so? I've heard a great deal about the Federal league.
Phil Yes, it's been given lots of newspaper space.
Ban Great place Jim's got here.
Phil Wonderful. They say nobody can touch his maple nut sundaes.
Ban Why can't we try one?
Phil We can to waiter. Please bring up two of Jim's specials.
Ban Much dancing around St. Louis?
Phil Yes, indeed. I'm at it every night.
Ban Are they still fox trotting?
Phil Well, they call it that, but really it looks to me like a combination of the old two-step, polka, and gallop. They've cut out the trotting part.
Ban They have in Chicago, too.
Phil Here's our sundaes.
Ban tasting his'n My, they certainly are good.
Phil Grand!
Ban Heavenly!
Phil I suppose you've had an early spring in Chicago, as we have had here.
Ban Yes, it's been beautiful weather so far; fine for baseball.
Phil I think they ought to get baseball out of the courts and confine it to the ball field.
Ban I'm inclined to agree with you. Well, I must catch a train.
Phil Sorry you have to hurry. Maybe we'll meet again some time.
Ban I hope so, I'm sure. Good-by, Mr. Hall.
Phil Good-by, Mr. Thompson. Please to've metchu.
Scene—Fisher Building, Chicago.
Ban Well, Mr. Wall, what brings you away from St. Louis?
Phil O, just here on a little business.
Ban How's St. Louis?
Phil O, pretty dull. But I guess it's the same all over.
Ban Going to be here long?
Phil No, I go back tonight. I'd take the noon train, only I made a date with a friend of mine here to play a little golf.
Ban O, you're a golfer, too, are you?
Phil Yes, are you?
Ban Just a beginner, you might say.
Phil Well, I haven't been at it long, but I certainly do love it.
Ban There's no better diversion for a man who's generally shut up in an office.
Phil Couldn't you join us this afternoon?
Ban No, I'm sorry, but I promised a friend of mine, Charley Comiskey, that I'd go out and see his baseball team play today. This is opening day. Very much like to have you go if you could get away from your golf.
Phil I'd like to go, at that. My fingers are so blistered I can hardly hold a club.
Ban Well, say you call up your friend and say you've changed your mind.
Phil I'll do that. I like to watch baseball.
Ban I do too. But I think they ought to get the game out of the courts and confine it to the field.
Phil I'm inclined to agree with you.

Act III

Scene—Comiskey Park.

Ban There's Charley Comiskey now.
Phil Comiskey. That's a funny name. Polish?
Ban No, no. He's of Irish descent.
Phil He's got a great plant here.
Ban Yes, and there's a great crowd here, too.
Phil It would probably be even larger if there were no baseball war.
Ban That's true. I think they ought to get the game out of the courts and confine it to the field.
Phil I'm inclined to agree with you.
Ban What do you say to an ice cream cone?
