The Fe-Mail Carrier

She rings the Browns' doorbell. Mrs. Brown opens the door.
Mrs. Brown Oh, good morning, dearie. Aren't you nearly frozen?
She I'm getting so I don't mind it. But I bet my nose is a sight.
Mrs. B. It doesn't look bad. Can't you come in?
She Not possibly. I'm terribly behind.
She enters the house and looks in the hall mirror.
Mrs. B. I don't suppose there's any mail for me.
She searches mail pouch and at length produces a powder puff, which she uses. I'll see in just a minute. She finishes powdering, replaces the puff and draws out a bundle of letters. She looks through them. Yes, here's something. But I'm afraid it's just a bill. She examines it. It's from the Economy Dry Goods, on Elston. She hands the letter to Mrs. Brown.
Mrs. B. examines both sides of an envelope Why, I haven't boughten anything there for weeks and weeks. They enter the living room and sit on the couch. This must be some mistake.
She It probably is. Some of these stores are terrible. I don't believe they keep any books. Why, way last May I—
Mrs. B. I can't imagine what this can be fore. I know I paid cash for that yarn.
She Maybe it isn't a bill. Maybe it's just a circular.
Mrs. Brown opens the envelope and takes out some advertising matter.
Mrs. B. Why, sure enough! It's just a circular. I was positive I didn't owe anything.
She glancing through a bundle of letters Is it Mrs. E. C. Hayes that lives next door?
Mrs. B. Yes. They moved in a week ago. I've only seen her once or twice, only once to speak to. She seems pleasant enough, but her clothes are a mess.
She examining a letter This is for her. It's from Danville.
Mrs. B. That's funny. I understood her to say she came from Wisconsin. But maybe I'm mistaken. No, I'm sure she said Wisconsin.
She Well, anyway, this is postmarked Danville. It's a woman's writing.
Mrs. B. leans over and looks at the letter Sure enough, it is. Danville, Illinois. Maybe she has relatives there.
She Or it may be her husband's relations.
Mrs. B. She told me where her husband's home is, but I forget.
She She may have a married sister in Danville.
Mrs. B. She didn't say anything about her.
She looking at another letter And here's one from Rockford for Mrs. Robert Cain.
Mrs. B. Oh, yes, her son, Tom, is at Camp Grant. He writes Bertha once a week.
She Isn't the war awful?
Mrs. B. Simply terrible!
She rising Well, I can't stay forever. I'm way behind now.
Mrs. B. Oh, before you go, I want to show you what I'm knitting. It's a sweater.
She sitting down again Oh, are you? I am, too. Look! She digs into mail pouch and produces her knitting.
Mrs. B. Oh! Why it's almost exactly the same color as mine. Wait till I get it.
She waits.
