Shall They Have the Vote?

Scene I

Monday p.m. Lobby of Auditorium theater. Crowd awaiting turns to buy tickets.

Lady at head of line What is the opera Saturday?
Box Office Man Afternoon or evening?
Lady Afternoon
B. O. Man "Romeo and Juliet."
Lady With Muratore and Galli-Serky?
B. O. Man Yes, it's a repetition.
Lady The same as last Friday night?
B. O. Man Yes.
Lady What are the prices?
B. O. Man Five dollars for lower floor.
Lady I wanted two—my sister's coming with me.
B. O. Man We only have singles.
Lady I wouldn't want singles.
B. O. Man I'm sorry.
Lady You say you have singles?
B. O. Man Yes; here's one in the fourteenth row.
Lady Is that the best you have?
B. O. Man Yes.
Lady Well, I'd have to have two together.
B. O. Man I'm sorry.
Lady going out My sister's coming with me.

Scene II

Monday p.m. A canned music shop. Crowd awaiting attention.

Lady I wonder if you have any Kreisler records.
Salesman Oh, yes.
Lady I wonder if you have that little thing he played last time. It was an encore. It began with an S.
Salesman Perhaps it's in the catalog. Just look under Kreisler.
Lady Would you kindly look for me? I'd have to put on my glasses. It began with an S.
Salesman opens catalog and reads the list of Kreisler records.
Lady No; it's none of those. It was just a little thing. He played it for an encore. I'm positive it began with an S, but I may be mistaken.
Salesman I'm afraid we can't find it unless you have the name of it.
Lady It was a pretty little thing; sort of simple. He plays so many things that really have no melody. This was an encore. It began with S.
Salesman I don't think we have it.
Lady Do you know another store where I'd be likely to find it?
Salesman I'm afraid not.
Lady Well, I'll have to try somewhere else.
She goes out.
