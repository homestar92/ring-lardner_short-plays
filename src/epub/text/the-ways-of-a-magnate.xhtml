The Ways of a Magnate

In Three Acts

Act I

A magnate's office. The reporters are paying him their daily visit. It is November.

Magnate "Well, boys, there isn't much in the way of news today. I got a letter from George Smart, who I think is one of the best judges of a ball player in the country, and he tells me tha twe made no mistake when we bought Luke McGluke from Wichita. He says Luke is the best young pitching prospect he's ever seen, that he's got more speed than Johnson, a spitball that breaks better than Walsh's, and a fadeaway more deceptive than Matty's. I am tickled to death to get McGluke because we just needed one more star righthander to round out our staff. Manager Smith and I believe that his acquisition means the pennant for us."

Act II

The same. The reporters are again calling on the magnate. It is February.

A Reporter "Well, I see the Feds have signed McGluke."
Magnate "Yes, and they can have him. With a pitching staff as strong as ours, he wouldn't have had a chance. Manager Smith and I were wondering what minor league club we could bunk into buying him and taking him off our hands. According to George Smart, he was greatly overrated, and I don't see how he's going to win even in a bush league like the Federal, which never will start the season."

Act III

The same. The reporters are again calling. It is later in February.

Magnate "I am informed by Henry Stingu, the ablest lawyer in the state, that the highest court will uphold our claim to McGluke. So I guess he'll play with us or not at all. I have sent him a contract and I expect him to sign. If he insists on going with the Feds, I will enjoin him from playing with them. But, of course, they'll never start the season."
