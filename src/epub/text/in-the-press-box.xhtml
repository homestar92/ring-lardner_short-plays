In the Press Box

Scene—Press Box at the Huntington Avenue Ball Grounds. In the distance, a score or so of men, two of them dressed in blue, half of the remainder in white and the other half in gray, are directing and playing a game known as baseball. Surrounding the field on which these men are at play, are bleachers and stands, well-filled with spectators, who, now and again, make noises of one kind and another. Time—An afternoon in July.

Jim

Well, baseball writers, journalists and liars,
What will you have before you get your wires?

Paul Some ginger ale.
Carl The same.
Nick Give me the same.
Paul We've got to keep our heads clear for the game.
Bill leaves his seat and approaches John I.
Bill

Most worthy sir, my paper wants to know
If you are selling Speaker. Is it so?

John I. No, no.
Lard leaves his seat and approaches John I.
Lard

My paper fain would ascertain through me
If you'll trade Joseph Wood for Birdie Cree.

John I. No, No.
Tim

The game is on. Take notice gents, I pray
How speedily Joe shoots them through to-day.

Sam

Ah, yes, he's fast but his best speed looks slow
Compared with what Columbus used to show.

O'leary

Ah, Christopher could surely whip them through
In those great days of fourteen-ninety-two.

Chorus Of Old Coves

Oh, take us back to those old days once more,
Those grand old days, those halcyon days of yore.

Eddie Singing

Come, josephine take a jab of morphine
Going up, she goes, she goes.

Francis Eaton

If I for you another song would buy,
Dear Eddie, could you learn it? Would you try?

Eddie singing

Come, mary Jane, take a sniff of cocaine.
Going up, she goes, up she goes.

Halloran What inning is it? Anybody know?
All No, no, no, no, no, no, no, no, no, no.
In the distance, a young man with an innocent smile and red undergarments steps forward, swinging a stick of wood. He hits a small white ball and starts running
John I. Go on for three. Two sacks are not enough.
Mcnulty

Go on, you, Lewis. Oh, go on, you Duff.
O-o-o-h. Falls out of his chair.

Kane Don't hold him there at second. Let him come.
Jim O'Loughlin's called him out, the fat-head bum.
O'leary Another question, gentlemen, I pray: How did you score that most disgusting play?
Tim A double for Duff Lewis.
Sam I guess not. Sam Crawford should have gobbled up that swat.
Paul A single and an error for Ty Cobb.
Nick That shows how much you know about the job. A sacrifice is what he ought to get.
Watts A boot for Delehanty is my bet.
Hoey A kick for Mullin and a double-play.
O'leary What have the balance of you got to say?
Webb A home run and a triple goes for me
Francis Eaton I say a muff for Moriarty.
Carl A wild pitch and a passed ball on my sheet.
Jim An error for O'Loughlin. He's a cheat.
Bill dictating Lewis lambasted a longitudinal lallapaloosa to left, turned second like a turbulent tornado, dashed devilishly for third and was capsized by Cobb's cantankerous throw.
Warmunde Spell it all out.
Lard dictating John I. Taylor doubled to left centre, and was caught trying to stretch it for a triple, Ban Johnson to Jack Gleason. Wait a minute. Did I say that right?
Mclean No, but I sent it right.
O'leary

Once more, tell me, kind sirs, I pray
How did you score that most distinguished play?

All hollering as loud as possible Passed ball. Error for Cobb. Double for Navin. Wild pitch for Jennings. Home run for Johnny Evers. Sacrifice hit for Taft.
Chorus Of Old Coves Oh, take us back to those old days once more; Those days when scorers knew the way to score.
Eddie Up, up, a little bit higher.
Sam How are the Rustlers coming out in Chi?
Jim Well, I can't tell you. Sorry, Hope to die, The wires are down--just where I do not know, It's somewhere west-southwest of Buffalo.
Francis Eaton Is anybody present keeping score? I've got two put-outs, just need twenty-four.
Halloran What inning is it? I would like to know.
Thomas Just look up at the board. 'Twill always show. See there, Bill Carrigan just fouled one off And did you hear that bleacher fellow cough? First time today, I'm very sure of that, That some one coughed with Carrigan at bat.
John I. Well, here's the way I've got the thing doped out. The Tigers are ahead, without a doubt. The score is 5 to 4, one round to go, And is that all you fellows want to know?
All And is that all, you ask? Why, man alive, Go on and tell us how they got their five. And when you've told us that, there's still some more, You'll have to tell us how we got our four.
John I. Well, in the op'ning inning, up came Cobb. He singled; Wagner wasn't on the job. The next three bases stolen, sad but true. The gate receipts, I guess he stole them too. In the third inning, up he came once more, With Bush on second waiting chance to score. Our peerless outfield tenders turned their backs, By Ty, he smashed it to the railroad tracks. And, in the sixth, with Bush once more on base, He punched the Bull a wallop in the face. That's how they got their five, a dirty shame. Our four they spotted us before the game. And as Cobb's coming up once more, good-day, I'm going ere he takes the park away.
Exit John I. The rest work hard and are still working to get something on their score books when the players and spectators leave the field.
O'leary Before you leave, come, gentlemen, I pray, Tell me, how did you score that Lewis play?
All The game is really over. Ain't it grand? We'll stick around and listen to the band.
Percy But wait a minute, boys, before you go, What was the final score?
All How should we know? We'll find out in the sporting extras later. There's still some stuff in that refrigerator.
Chorus Of Old Coves Oh, take us back to those good old days again, Those days when baseball writers still were sane.
Eddie Come, Josephine.
Exeunt.
