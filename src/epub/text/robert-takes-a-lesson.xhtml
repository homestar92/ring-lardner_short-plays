Robert Takes a Lesson

Idea Swiped from Walter McCornack

Scene—Harvard Stadium. Time—Next September. The Harvard squad is at practice, Prof. Haughton enters, leading little Robert Zuppke.

Prof. Haughton Those are the candidates for my team. What are they?
Robert The candidates for your team.
Prof. Haughton Good. There are 125 of them. How many are there?
Robert One hundred and twenty-five. Where re the rest of them?
Prof. Haughton Probably busy in class. Probably what?
Robert Busy in class.
Prof. Haughton See that yellow oval object the man is kicking? What is it?
Robert I dunno. What is it?
Prof. Haughton That's the football. That other man is now punting it. When he drops the ball from his hands and kicks it before it touches the ground it's a punt. What is it?
Robert A bunt.
Prof. Haughton No, no. Not a bunt. A punt.
Robert What is that squad of men doing at the other end of the field?
Prof. Haughton That's a team practicing signals.
Robert Is that the Harvard team?
Prof. Haughton Not Team No. 1. That's Team No. 7, the team that plays Maine, Bates, and Brown. The team practicing in the middle of the field is Team No. 2, that plays Princeton and Cornell. Pretty soon Team No. 2, that plays Yale, will run through its signals.
Robert Where is Team No. 1?
Prof. Haughton I'm giving No. 1 a rest. I don't expect ot have to use it this season. We don't play any western schools.
Robert Is that man throwing the ball?
Prof. Haughton Yes, that's just what he's doing; only we call it passing the ball. He's practicing the forward pass.
Robert Is that legal?
Prof. Haughton O, yes. It's a play eastern teams are using extensively. You'll probably see it in th west in five or six years.
Robert Who is that man peeking round the corner of the stand?
Prof. Haughton Ah! I hand't noticed him. Thanks. He blows a whistle and an orderly dashes up. Orderly, there's a newspaper man behind the stand. Summon Companies 4 and 7 of the Cavalry Bouncers and see that he is properly dealt with.
Robert What are those white lines on the ground?
Prof. Haughton Those are the ten yard lines, to aid the players and officials in determining how much ground must be gained. What are they?
Robert The ten yard lines to—to—to—
Prof. Haughton To aid—
Robert To aid the officials—
Prof. Haughton The players and officials—
Robert In determining how much ground must be gained.
Prof. Haughton Good. See those upright posts connected by a cross bar at either end of the field?
Robert Yes.
Prof. Haughton Those are the goal posts. When the football is placed, kicked, or drop-kicked over one of those cross bars and between those posts by a Harvard man, it's a goal. What is it?
Robert A goal. And what is it if the ball is kicked by other than a Harvard man?
Prof. Haughton An accident.
Robert Do accidents often happen?
Prof. Haughton Occasionally. But we are rapidly doing away with them.
Robert How?
Prof. Haughton By dropping the offenders from our schedule.
Robert But surely teams should not be punished for an accident.
Prof. Haughton Nevertheless they are. An upstart named Mollwitz or Molebite or something like that accidentally gained two or three hundred yards through the line of Team No. 1½ last fall and the eleven with which he played was dropped like a hot cake.
An Orderly advances.
Orderly Your Highness, thirty-six assistant coaches are waiting outside.
Prof. Haughton Tell them practice will be over in four minutes.
The Orderly exits.
Robert I wish we could get a game with your Team No. 8 or 9.
Prof. Haughton Whom do you mean by "we"?
Robert Illinois.
Prof. Haughton Illinois? Illinois? O, yes, I believe I passed through that place on the way to Chicago. How strong are you?
Robert We're about the best in the west.
Prof. Haughton As strong as Michigan?
Robert I believe so.
Prof. Haughton Do you see that gate marked "Exit"?
Robert Yes.
Prof. Haughton. Sorry you can't stick round a while longer, old top. But I've got to teach Team No. 12 a new play called the crisscross, so that they can work it against Team No. 2. Yale invented the play last week and we must learn to stop it. Of course, I couldn't let you see it.
Robert I believe I've already seen it.
Prof. Haughton Where?
Robert In high school games out west.
Prof. Haughton blows his whistle. Robert exits in haste.
